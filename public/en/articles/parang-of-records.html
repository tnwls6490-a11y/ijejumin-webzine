Came to Jeju with 500,000 won, stayed to live better

Lim Ha-ram, photographer and owner of Parang of Records, on moving to Jeju and the time spent settling in

Oct 2, 2026

Lim Ha-ram

500,000 won

Gangjeong-dong, Seogwipo

A photographer who runs a photo studio and a goods shop in Jeju

  • Starting with 500,000 won
  • A seven-colored rainbow
  • Love

Lim left office life and came down to Jeju as if running away, carrying 500,000 won. Lim saved money working as a guesthouse manager, then opened a photo studio and a goods shop, creating work of Lim's own. At first, Lim says, it was about coming here to get by; now it is about staying on to live better.

The flow of people moving into and out of Jeju has kept changing. In 2010, those who moved in outnumbered those who moved out by 437. In 2015, that gap widened to some 14,000, and in 2025, by contrast, 4,273 more people moved out than moved in.

Statistics: Honam Regional Statistics Office, “Jeju Population Migration Trends”; Ministry of Data and Statistics, “2025 Annual Jeju Regional Economic Trends”

The gap between move-ins and move-outs alone can hardly tell us why a person came to Jeju, or what kind of time that person has spent here while staying on.

Six years in Jeju. Photographer Lim Ha-ram, too, had not dreamed of life in Jeju from the start. Going through a breakup while still working at a company, Lim said, “I'm going to run away to the place farthest from you.”

When Lim set off for Jeju like that, the money in hand was 500,000 won.

CHAPTER 01

A month later, a place Lim wanted to live in

Settling in Jeju was not the plan at first. But after a month on the island Lim had come to as if running away, Lim's feelings changed. Living here, Lim thought, might be all right.

“This place was more beautiful than I was. I think I felt that if I lived here, I could become beautiful too.”

— Lim Ha-ram, photographer and owner of Parang of Records

Lim had come to want to keep living in Jeju, but building a foundation for daily life was another challenge.

Without the money to rent a room right away, Lim worked as a guesthouse manager for six months. With room and board taken care of, Lim saved money and later rented a room, paying the rent a year at a time. Lim also kept going by working part-time jobs.

Looking back on those early days of settling in, Lim talks about how much a preparation period matters.

“If you don't have money, working at a guesthouse that pays a monthly salary is a good option too. With about six months to prepare, I didn't have any big problems getting by afterward.”

Finding a job to stay in for the long term was not easy. While job hunting, Lim said, being someone who had moved to Jeju from elsewhere sometimes felt like a barrier.

CHAPTER 02

Opening a photo studio, making work of one's own

In search of a way to make a living, Lim prepared to start a business. Through the New Business Startup Academy, Lim prepared to open a photo studio, and was later also selected for the Strong Small Business program, gradually building work of Lim's own.

The first place Lim opened was Parang Photo Studio in Ara-dong. ‘Parang’ comes from parang (波浪), a word meaning waves. The name carries the idea of stirring up one small wave in Jeju.

After the photo studio, Lim also came to run a goods shop, Parang of Records. Each of the two spaces was given one color of the rainbow. Parang Photo Studio is blue, and Parang of Records is green. Lim's goal is to fill in the stores all the way to a seventh, violet one, completing a seven-colored rainbow in Jeju.

As the stores grew from one to two, so did the burden of the work.

“The hardest thing about running a business, in the end, is my own mental state. Don't go into business lightly. Run just one business.”

Even while speaking of how hard business is, Lim has no thought of leaving Jeju. For Lim, Jeju is a place that offers the chance to keep doing the work Lim loves.

CHAPTER 03

Meeting people, getting to know my neighborhood

The people Lim met in Jeju also shaped how Lim settled in. Lim saw people who had seemed curt at first become attentive and caring once they grew close. Through those relationships, Lim says, even the way of treating people changed.

The way Lim sees Jeju changed, too. Where Lim once mostly visited famous tourist spots, the interest now turns to Lim's own neighborhood and the community within it.

After Yeon-dong and Yongdam, Lim has now lived in Gangjeong-dong, Seogwipo, for more than two years. Lim picked Yongdam as a good place to clear one's head while driving along the coastal road. Gangjeong-dong, where Lim lives now, is well kept, clean and easy to live in, Lim said.

When the commute feels hard, Lim sometimes turns off toward an oreum (Jeju's volcanic cones) or Hallasan to take in the scenery on the way. Even in a daily routine of working and coming home, there is time to enjoy in Jeju.

CHAPTER 04

The reason for staying in Jeju: love

This is how Lim Ha-ram describes the difference between first coming to Jeju and now.

“I came here in the first place to get by, and now I'm staying to live better.”

— Lim Ha-ram

Life has changed shape, from a time of getting through each day to a time of carrying on with the work Lim wants to do.

“If at first it was a desperate struggle, now I feel like I want to run and fly.”

Asked to name in one word the reason for carrying on life in Jeju, Lim answered.

“Love.”

A 10-month-old child, family, and the work Lim loves. Jeju is now the place where Lim builds a better life together with all of them.

NOTE

Three things to look into when preparing to settle in Jeju

1. A preparation period for your early days

Lim covered room and board and saved money by working at a guesthouse. Use that experience as a reference, but think about the preparation period you need in light of your own situation. You should also look into where to stay right after the move and how you will cover living costs.

2. Support programs that fit the work you plan to do

Lim prepared for a business of Lim's own through a startup support program. You can look into support programs and startup programs that interest you in advance, and tie their eligibility requirements and preparation process into your moving plans.

3. Housing and your moving schedule

Jeju has a custom of moving house during the Singugan period. Keep it in mind, but it is better to decide when to move while checking actual listings and lease terms. If you are considering joining a support program, check its application schedule as well.

Source: Jeju Tourism Organization, Visit Jeju, “Folk Customs”
