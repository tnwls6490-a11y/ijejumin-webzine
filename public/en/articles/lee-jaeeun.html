Working and living in Jeju: Baengnok Marketing CEO Lee Jae-eun’s five years

From music to marketing: the story of CEO Lee Jae-eun, who rebuilt their way of working and living in Jeju

Oct 9, 2026

CEO running Baengnok Marketing in Jeju

  • From music to marketing
  • Three years of trial and error

A musician who had played in a band for about 10 years, Lee switched paths to marketing and, on a vacation in Jeju, decided to move there. After three years of trial and error, they went back to what they do best, online marketing, and now run their current company.

A life of working freely wherever you want, without being tied to a company. It is something everyone dreams of at least once, yet it is hard to reach.

Baengnok Marketing CEO Lee Jae-eun, whom we met in Jeju, was the person who came closest to that kind of life.

But that life was not handed to Lee from the start.

CHAPTER 01

Even for the same work, somewhere a little more like me

Lee’s starting point was not marketing but music.

They had long dreamed of becoming a musician and kept playing in a band for about 10 years, but making a stable living from music alone came with practical difficulties.

Weighing a new path, they stepped into marketing and built hands-on experience in Seoul, moving between agencies and in-house roles.

Amid a fast-moving routine, their days were always hectic.

The turning point came during a vacation in Jeju.

One night, during one of several trips back and forth to Jeju, Lee was moved by the scenery on the way back to their lodging after finishing work at a café.

“If I had to work late into the night anyway, like in Seoul, I thought, wouldn’t it be better to work somewhere I could feel even a little more at ease?”

— Lee Jae-eun, CEO of Baengnok Marketing

Lee set the goal of starting a business with a new idea and decided to go to Jeju.

They looked into startup support programs and received support, and also made use of programs for young people, such as help with moving costs and monthly rent.

The information needed to settle in came through Jeju-area open chat rooms, the Center for Creative Economy and Innovation, and people they met locally.

It was a move carefully prepared in order to turn a dream into reality.

But coming to Jeju did not change reality on its own.

Lee had expected a relaxed routine of running, swimming and climbing Hallasan every day, but the biggest task they actually faced was making a living.

No one paid them just because they had come to Jeju, and the new idea-based startup they tried did not go as planned either.

The trial and error went on for about three years.

Throughout that time, Lee kept asking themself the same question.

‘What is the problem?’

CHAPTER 02

What only became clear after a three-year detour

The answer was back at the starting point.

Instead of following what others were doing, Lee looked back again at what they truly wanted and what kind of work they did best.

As they thought about wanting to be a genuine person with real expertise, what came to mind was the marketing experience they had built up in Seoul.

In their third year in Jeju, Lee went back to what they do best, online marketing, and set up their current company.

Most of the work comes in by word of mouth.

Some jobs began when an acquaintance asked, ‘Is it true you’re the best at marketing in Jeju?’ and the reply was, ‘Yes, that’s me.’

What Lee considers most important is not immediate sales figures but the results they show their clients. That is because they believe that producing results through good planning and execution naturally leads to the next opportunity.

To keep the company running steadily, they offer subscription-based marketing and regular consulting alongside one-off projects.

They also set their own principles for the way they work.

Cut unnecessary travel and repetitive work, and focus more on what matters.

Back when there were still employees, Lee moved meetings and work online, and makes active use of AI for meeting notes and organizing materials.

By managing documents and files with Claude, they also reduced the time and effort the work takes.

These changes led to freedom in where to work.

Without having to stay in an office, they can now keep working from home, or sometimes from a café with a view of the sea.

That does not mean every meeting is replaced with an online one.

Considering that clients in Jeju tend to prefer face-to-face communication, Lee holds the first meeting in person and collaborates online after that, looking after both efficiency and clients’ trust.

They named the density of competition as the biggest difference between Seoul and Jeju.

“In Seoul there are so many people that sometimes, even when I do well, it looks as if I didn’t. In Jeju there are so few people that if I do even a little well, it looks like I did well, and that gives me more drive.”

— Lee Jae-eun

Lee said that, compared with their annual salary when they worked in Seoul, their revenue has now grown more than tenfold.

Of course, this is one person’s experience, and it is likely the result not only of regional characteristics but also of the experience, client relationships and way of running the business they have built up.

CHAPTER 03

The conditions for turning Jeju’s romance into reality

Lee said that Jeju may look like a fantasy island full of romance, but once you actually come, it is not quite like that.

That does not mean there is anything wrong with Jeju.

The problem was the way of coming here with eyes only for the romance, without preparing.

What Lee recommends first to people dreaming of life in Jeju is, surprisingly, something practical: stability in ‘clothing, food and shelter.’

If the basics of what you wear, what you eat and where you stay are shaky, it is hard to last long.

Lee, too, started out in a studio apartment and now lives in a 30-pyeong (about 99 m²) apartment, and said that once life became stable, work efficiency rose noticeably.

So Lee’s advice is clear.

Rather than coming to Jeju on a whim, first look back at what kind of person you are.

Next, they said, consider whether that person is a good fit for Jeju, and find out in advance what you need to prepare in order to adapt to Jeju.

Lee advised looking for those answers not just by thinking them over in your head but by actually living in Jeju. The point is to experience in advance whether it is the right environment for you, and what work you could do to build a life there.

The five years in Jeju were not a time of simply living out the romance, but a time of finding ways to carry that romance into everyday life.
